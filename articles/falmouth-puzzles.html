Find it on the map

A walking scavenger hunt drawn from the same history facts. Private homes and private waterfront communities are marked so you know what to view from a public way only. Unfold the pull-out map or open the history layer on the discovery map.

  1. The civic heart

    Find it Downtown side A1 (also drawn on the Woods Hole side at C2).

    Stand on the Village Green, where Main Street meets Palmer Avenue and Town Hall Square. Falmouth was settled in 1660 and incorporated in 1686. Town Hall itself is Downtown side B2.

  2. The hymn writer, from the sidewalk

    Find it Downtown side B1.

    16 Main Street, on the Green, is Katharine Lee Bates’s birthplace. It is a private residence. View it from the sidewalk and do not approach the door. Then walk to her statue on the library lawn, Downtown side C2.

  3. From sea to shining sea

    Find it Downtown side A2 (Woods Hole stretch: Woods Hole side B3).

    Find a paved access to the Shining Sea Bikeway, the 10.7-mile path named for Bates’s line, on the old railroad bed between Falmouth and Woods Hole.

  4. A light on the sound

    Find it Woods Hole side B4.

    Nobska Light, 233 Nobska Road, where the sounds meet and Martha’s Vineyard sits on the horizon. The station dates to 1828; the present tower to 1876.

  5. Science on the harbor

    Find it Woods Hole side A3 (WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and Eel Pond are in A4).

    The Woods Hole Science Aquarium is at 166 Water Street. A WHOI team helped find the Titanic in 1985. The aquarium is closed through early 2027 for foundation repairs; the village waterfront is still the place to stand with the story.

  6. Seven miles, two squares

    Find it Start: Woods Hole side A3. Finish: Downtown side E3.

    The Falmouth Road Race starts on Water Street in Woods Hole and finishes at the ballfield in Falmouth Heights. You do not have to run the seven miles; just find both ends on the map.

  7. Where the Casino stood

    Find it Downtown side E3.

    On Grand Avenue in the Heights, between the ballfield and the beach, stood the Casino by the Sea, demolished in 2003. The square is the same one as the race finish.

  8. The house floats, from shore

    Find it Woods Hole side A4.

    The Woods Hole house floats lie in Great Harbor near Ram Island. This is a private summer community. View it only from the public waterfront or the water; do not approach the floats.

  9. The church that strawberries built

    Find it Off the pull-out map — East Falmouth.

    St. Anthony’s Church, 167 East Falmouth Highway, opened on January 1, 1924, after Portuguese strawberry growers set aside harvest money for a parish. It sits east of the illustrated Downtown and Woods Hole sheets.

  10. A hangar for a garage

    Find it Off the pull-out map — East Falmouth.

    Falmouth Airpark, 67 Airpark Drive, is a residential fly-in neighborhood. It is private. Do not treat it as a walking tour; find it on a town map and leave the taxiways to the people who live there.
