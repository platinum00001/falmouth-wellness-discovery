Falmouth Stories

Katharine Lee Bates’s birthplace

The author of “America the Beautiful” was born on August 12, 1859, in the Congregational parsonage at 16 Main Street, on the Village Green.

B1

Katharine Lee Bates’s birthplace

16 Main Street, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side B1 — On the Village Green, near First Congregational Church (Downtown B1) and the Green (Downtown A1).

See this pin on the discovery map

Katharine Lee Bates was born on August 12, 1859, at 16 Main Street, then the parsonage rented by First Congregational Church for its minister, Rev. William Bates, and his family. The house itself dates to 1810. The Falmouth Historical Society bought the birthplace in 1976 and later sold it, in October 2008; it is a private residence today. Bates wrote the lines of “America the Beautiful” after a visit to Pikes Peak in 1893, taught English at Wellesley College, and is buried in Oak Grove Cemetery in Falmouth. The public places to spend time with her story are the statue on the Falmouth Public Library lawn and the collections at the library and at Falmouth Museums on the Green.

If you go: 16 Main Street is a private residence. Please view it from the sidewalk and do not approach the door.

Nearby partner: Palmer House Inn — 81 Palmer Avenue, a short walk from the Village Green.

Learn more: First Congregational Church of Falmouth, Our History

Katharine Lee Bates statue

A bronze statue of Bates, by sculptor Lloyd Lillie, stands on the lawn of Falmouth Public Library at 300 Main Street.

C2

Katharine Lee Bates statue

Falmouth Public Library lawn, 300 Main Street, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side C2 — On the library lawn, Downtown side C2; the library also appears on the Woods Hole side at D1.

See this pin on the discovery map

A life-size bronze statue of Katharine Lee Bates stands on the lawn of Falmouth Public Library, 300 Main Street. Sculptor Lloyd Lillie portrayed her at 34, at Pikes Peak, the moment that led to “America the Beautiful.” The statue was unveiled on August 12, 1986, and dedicated on August 12, 1988, during Falmouth’s tricentennial. The library keeps a collection of her work in the Lemcke Reference Room. From the lawn it is a short walk along Main Street (Downtown D2) to the shops already in this issue.

If you go: The statue stands on the public library lawn at 300 Main Street. Check the library’s current hours if you want to see the Bates collection inside.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Public Library — The statue is on the library lawn at 300 Main Street.

Learn more: Falmouth Public Library, Special Collections

Falmouth Stories

Shining Sea Bikeway

A 10.7-mile paved rail trail from Woods Hole on Vineyard Sound to North Falmouth on Buzzards Bay, named for a line in “America the Beautiful.”

A2

Shining Sea Bikeway (Depot Avenue access)

Shining Sea Bikeway at Depot Avenue, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side A2 — Downtown access is at A2 (the path also passes the Depot area in A1). The Woods Hole stretch is on the Woods Hole side at B3.

See this pin on the discovery map

The Shining Sea Bikeway is a 10.7-mile paved path on the old New York, New Haven and Hartford Railroad bed, which carried trains between Falmouth and Woods Hole from 1872 to 1965. The town dedicated the first stretch as a bicentennial project in 1975 and extended the trail to North Falmouth in 2009. The name honors Katharine Lee Bates and the line “from sea to shining sea.” It is the only bikeway on Cape Cod that runs alongside the sea. The town maintains it for cycling, walking, jogging, skating, and cross-country skiing.

If you go: The pin marks the downtown access near Depot Avenue, a short walk from Corner Cycle on Palmer Avenue. The path continues south toward Woods Hole along Surf Drive.

Nearby partner: Corner Cycle — 115 Palmer Avenue, by the downtown end of the bikeway.

Learn more: Town of Falmouth, The Shining Sea Bikeway

Woods Hole science and the Titanic discovery

WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and the nation’s oldest public marine aquarium share the Woods Hole waterfront. In 1985, a WHOI-led team helped find the wreck of RMS Titanic.

A3

Woods Hole Science Aquarium (WHOI, MBL, and the Titanic story)

166 Water Street, Woods Hole, MA 02543

Woods Hole side A3 — The aquarium is at A3; WHOI, the Marine Biological Laboratory, and Eel Pond are in A4, just along the waterfront.

See this pin on the discovery map

Woods Hole, a village of Falmouth, is one of the country’s major marine-science centers. The Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution (WHOI), the Marine Biological Laboratory, and NOAA’s Woods Hole Science Aquarium sit along the village waterfront. Spencer Baird established a U.S. Fish Commission research station here in 1871; a public research aquarium was added in 1875, and NOAA calls it the nation’s oldest public marine aquarium, at 166 Water Street. On September 1, 1985, a joint expedition of WHOI and France’s IFREMER, with WHOI’s Dr. Robert D. Ballard and IFREMER’s Jean-Louis Michel, found the wreck of RMS Titanic. After the French ship Le Suroît narrowed the search, WHOI’s research vessel Knorr, using the deep-towed imaging system Argo, followed a debris trail and identified a Titanic boiler. The Knorr returned to Woods Hole on September 9, 1985, to a waterfront welcome. The aquarium is the public doorway to this story — and as of November 2026 it is closed through early 2027 for foundation repairs and exhibit work.

If you go: The Woods Hole Science Aquarium at 166 Water Street is closed through early 2027 for repairs. Check NOAA’s visit page for the reopening date. WHOI and the Marine Biological Laboratory are working research campuses; enjoy the village waterfront and official visitor information rather than walking into labs.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Transport — A ride to Woods Hole and the ferry terminal; call (774) 247-5263.

Learn more: WHOI, 1985 discovery of RMS Titanic

Nobska Light

The light station at Woods Hole was established in 1828; the present tower dates to 1876 and looks out over Vineyard Sound toward Martha’s Vineyard.

B4

Nobska Light

233 Nobska Road, Woods Hole, MA 02543

Woods Hole side B4 — On the Woods Hole side of the pull-out at B4.

See this pin on the discovery map

Nobska Light stands at the southwestern tip of Cape Cod in Woods Hole, at 233 Nobska Road, where Buzzards Bay, Vineyard Sound, and Nantucket Sound meet, with Martha’s Vineyard and Nonamesset Island in the view. The light station was established in 1828; the present iron tower, with a brick lining, was built in 1876. It was automated in 1985 and listed on the National Register of Historic Places in 1987. The Town of Falmouth and the Friends of Nobska Light care for the station; volunteer-led tours and a museum shop run seasonally. The Falmouth Road Race course passes the light in its first mile, and the Shining Sea Bikeway offers a winter walk in this direction (Woods Hole side B3).

If you go: The grounds are a public landmark at 233 Nobska Road, Woods Hole. Tower tours and the museum shop are seasonal — check Friends of Nobska Light for current hours before you go.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Transport — A ride out to Woods Hole and Nobska; call (774) 247-5263.

Learn more: Friends of Nobska Light

Falmouth Road Race, start and finish

Since August 15, 1973, this seven-mile race has run from the Cap’n Kidd on Water Street in Woods Hole to Falmouth Heights.

A3

Falmouth Road Race start

Water Street at the Cap’n Kidd, 77 Water Street, Woods Hole, MA 02543

Woods Hole side A3 — On Water Street in Woods Hole village (A3), next to Eel Pond and the drawbridge in A4.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Transport — A ride to the Woods Hole start; call (774) 247-5263.

See this pin on the discovery map

E3

Falmouth Road Race finish

Central Park, Falmouth Heights (between Central Park Avenue and the Finish Line Garden), Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side E3 — In Falmouth Heights, Downtown side E3, between the ballfield and Falmouth Heights Beach.

Nearby partner: Holiday Cycles — 465 Grand Avenue, in Falmouth Heights near the finish.

See this pin on the discovery map

The Falmouth Road Race began on August 15, 1973, the 40th birthday of Woods Hole bartender Tommy Leonard. He organized a run from the Cap’n Kidd on Water Street in Woods Hole to Falmouth Heights, first as a fund-raiser, and the course has been a fixture of the running calendar ever since. The route is about seven miles: from Water Street over the Eel Pond drawbridge, past Nobska Light, along Surf Drive, around Falmouth Inner Harbor, and up into the Heights. In the early years the finish was at the Brothers Four, a bar across from the Casino by the Sea; both of those buildings are gone. Today runners finish at the ballfield in Falmouth Heights, between Central Park Avenue and the Falmouth Road Race Finish Line Garden.

If you go: The start is on Water Street in Woods Hole, in front of the Cap’n Kidd. The finish is the public ballfield at Central Park in Falmouth Heights. The race itself is held in August; check falmouthroadrace.com for the current date and course details.

Learn more: Falmouth Road Race

Highfield Hall & Gardens

The Beebe family’s 1878 summer mansion at 56 Highfield Drive, now a restored public hall, gardens, and arts center beside Beebe Woods.

A1

Highfield Hall & Gardens

56 Highfield Drive, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side A1 — On the Downtown side at A1, in the same square as the Village Green and the north end of the bikeway area.

See this pin on the discovery map

Highfield Hall, at 56 Highfield Drive, was built in 1878 for the Beebe family of Boston. It is one of the early summer mansions on Cape Cod and a rare surviving example of Stick-style Queen Anne architecture in the Northeast. Boston architect Carl Fehmer designed the house. The estate once included a second mansion, Tanglewood, park-like gardens, and hundreds of acres of woodland. After decades of changing owners, Tanglewood was demolished in 1977, and Highfield stood neglected until a community restoration brought it back as a public hall for art, music, and gardens. In 1972, Josephine and Josiah K. Lilly III bought the estate and gave nearly 400 acres of Beebe Woods to the town for permanent conservation. The grounds are open daily, year-round, from dawn to dusk; the hall’s gallery hours are seasonal.

If you go: The gardens and grounds at 56 Highfield Drive are open daily from dawn to dusk. The hall’s gallery hours are seasonal — check highfieldhallandgardens.org before you go.

Nearby partner: Cape Symphony Conservatory — 60 Highfield Drive, next to Highfield Hall.

Learn more: Highfield Hall & Gardens, History

Falmouth Village Green, settled 1660, a town since 1686

English colonists settled Falmouth in 1660, and the town was incorporated in 1686. The Village Green remains the historic heart of Falmouth village.

A1

Falmouth Village Green

Falmouth Village Green, Main Street at Palmer Avenue and Town Hall Square, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side A1 — Downtown side A1; the Green is also drawn on the Woods Hole side at C2. Town Hall is in Downtown B2.

See this pin on the discovery map

The Secretary of the Commonwealth records that Falmouth was settled in 1660 and incorporated as a town in 1686. The Village Green, where Main Street meets Palmer Avenue and Town Hall Square, is still the civic and historic center of Falmouth village. First Congregational Church, gathered in 1708 and moved to its present spot at 68 Main Street in 1857, looks over the Green; a Paul Revere bell in its steeple still rings. Falmouth Town Hall stands on Town Hall Square, and Falmouth Museums on the Green occupy the historic houses just up Palmer Avenue. The Green is the right place to stand when you want to feel how old the town is, then walk east down Main Street or west toward the bikeway and Highfield.

If you go: The Village Green is a public park at Main Street and Palmer Avenue, open all year. Town Hall is at 59 Town Hall Square.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Town Hall — 59 Town Hall Square, on the Green.

Learn more: Massachusetts Secretary of the Commonwealth, city and town incorporation and settlement dates

Falmouth Museums on the Green

The Falmouth Historical Society’s campus at 55–65 Palmer Avenue, beside the Village Green, with historic houses, a research library, and gardens.

B1

Falmouth Museums on the Green

55–65 Palmer Avenue, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side B1 — On the Downtown side at B1, beside the Village Green and First Congregational Church.

See this pin on the discovery map

Falmouth Museums on the Green, the Falmouth Historical Society, occupy a campus at 55–65 Palmer Avenue, next to the Village Green. The visitor center is in Hallett Barn. The c. 1790 Dr. Francis Wicks House is the oldest house on the Town Green and is shown as a Federal-era physician’s home. The c. 1730 Conant House holds the society’s offices, exhibitions, and research library, including the exhibit “Falmouth: Changing with the Times.” The Falmouth Garden Club tends a colonial flower garden, an herb garden, and Memorial Park on the grounds. The Cultural Center, opened in 2012, hosts lectures and programs. This is the public museum for the town’s history — including the story of Katharine Lee Bates, whose nearby birthplace the society once owned.

If you go: The campus is at 55–65 Palmer Avenue. Office hours are listed as Monday–Friday, 10 a.m. to 3 p.m.; house tours and walking tours are seasonal. Call (508) 548-4857 or check museumsonthegreen.org for current hours. The gardens are open in daylight year-round.

Nearby partner: Palmer House Inn — 81 Palmer Avenue, a short walk from the museum campus.

Learn more: Falmouth Museums on the Green

The Woods Hole house floats

A small seasonal community of house floats in Great Harbor, moored near Ram Island and Devil’s Foot Island, reached only by boat — the only community of its kind on Cape Cod.

A4

Woods Hole house floats (Great Harbor)

Great Harbor, Woods Hole, near Ram Island and Devil’s Foot Island, Falmouth, MA 02543

Woods Hole side A4 — In Great Harbor, just off the illustrated Woods Hole shore at A4, toward Ram Island and Devil’s Foot Island.

See this pin on the discovery map

In Great Harbor, Woods Hole, a handful of house floats — cottages on pontoons, often called houseboats, though they have no engine of their own — form a small summer community. Town regulations allow them to be moored within 600 linear feet of Ram Island or Devil’s Foot Island in Great Harbor from mid-April to mid-November, and in Eel Pond for winter storage. The Falmouth Select Board and local reporting have described about 14 to 19 floats, and have called this the only community of its kind on Cape Cod. They can be seen from the Woods Hole waterfront; they are private seasonal homes, not a public landing.

If you go: This is a private seasonal community on the water, reached only by boat, and it is not open to visitors. Look from the public Woods Hole waterfront and Great Harbor shore; do not board, land on, or approach the floats.

Nearby partner: Falmouth Transport — A ride to the Woods Hole waterfront; call (774) 247-5263.

Learn more: Town of Falmouth, house float and houseboat regulations

Falmouth Airpark, the fly-in neighborhood

Massachusetts’ only residential fly-in community, in East Falmouth off Fresh Pond Road, where homes sit along taxiways around a 2,298-foot runway.

Off sheet

Falmouth Airpark

67 Airpark Drive, East Falmouth, MA 02536

Off the pull-out map — East Falmouth — North of Route 28, off Fresh Pond Road in East Falmouth. The illustrated pull-out covers Downtown–Main Street and Main Street & Woods Hole, so this neighborhood is off that sheet.

See this pin on the discovery map

Falmouth Airpark (FAA identifier 5B6) is a privately owned, public-use airport and residential fly-in community in East Falmouth, reached from Fresh Pond Road north of Route 28. The Falmouth Airpark Homeowners Association is at 67 Airpark Drive. Homes are sited along grass taxiways around a 2,298-by-40-foot runway, so residents can taxi an airplane from a hangar to the strip. Flying magazine has called it the only residential airpark in Massachusetts. The airport is listed by the FAA as open to the public, with posted rules for transient aircraft; the streets around it are a private residential neighborhood, not a walking tour.

If you go: This is a privately owned airport and a residential neighborhood. Do not walk the taxiways or treat the streets as a sightseeing stop. Transient aircraft should follow current FAA and airpark rules. The community is in East Falmouth, off the illustrated pull-out map.

Nearby partner: Route 28 Auto Center — 550 East Falmouth Highway, in East Falmouth south of the airpark.

Learn more: AirNav, Falmouth Airpark (5B6)

Falmouth Marine Railways and the Flying Bridge

Founded in 1940 as Falmouth Marine Railways, the boatyard at 278 Scranton Avenue shares Falmouth Inner Harbor with the Flying Bridge Restaurant at 220 Scranton Avenue.

D3

Falmouth Marine Railways (Falmouth Marine)

278 Scranton Avenue, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side D3 — On the west side of Falmouth Inner Harbor, Downtown D3, toward the harbor mouth and the Island Queen at E2.

See this pin on the discovery map

D3

Flying Bridge Restaurant

220 Scranton Avenue, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side D3 — On the west side of Falmouth Inner Harbor, Downtown D3, next to Falmouth Marine.

See this pin on the discovery map

Falmouth Marine & Yachting Center, at 278 Scranton Avenue on the west side of Falmouth Inner Harbor, was founded in 1940 and was originally known as Falmouth Marine Railways. The yard has served local boats for most of a century and, during World War II, work connected with the U.S. Navy. Just down the same harbor street, the Flying Bridge Restaurant occupies a waterfront building at 220 Scranton Avenue. Town assessing records list the restaurant building as dating to 1958; the Falmouth Chamber of Commerce says the restaurant was established in 1983. A claim that the restaurant dates to 1957 could not be verified, so that year is not used here. Together the railway-turned-yard and the restaurant tell the story of the inner harbor as a working and visiting waterfront.

If you go: Falmouth Marine is a working boatyard at 278 Scranton Avenue. The Flying Bridge Restaurant is next door at 220 Scranton Avenue; check the restaurant’s hours before you go. Windfall Market is farther up the same street at 77 Scranton Avenue.

Nearby partner: Windfall Market — 77 Scranton Avenue, on the same harbor street.

The old Casino by the Sea, Falmouth Heights

A Falmouth Heights landmark between the ballfield and the beach, built around 1901, rebuilt after a 1909 fire, and demolished in 2003.

E3

Former site of the Casino by the Sea

Grand Avenue at Falmouth Heights Beach, between Central Park and the water, Falmouth, MA 02540

Downtown side E3 — In Falmouth Heights, Downtown side E3, between the ballfield and Falmouth Heights Beach.

See this pin on the discovery map

For much of the twentieth century the Casino by the Sea stood on Grand Avenue in Falmouth Heights, sandwiched between the ballfield and Falmouth Heights Beach. The original building went up around the turn of the century (local reporting has used 1901), housed a post office in its early years, burned in 1909, and was rebuilt in a Dutch Colonial style by local builder William Tobey. Over time the first floor held a movie theater, shops, and a bathhouse; in the 1920s and 1930s the second floor was the Cottage Club. In 1966 it became the Casino by the Sea nightclub and restaurant, a summer gathering place. Falmouth Road Race runners came past it just before the finish, and crowds often ended up there, though the official early finish was at the Brothers Four, across the way. The Casino was demolished on November 25, 2003. The public places that remain are the beach, the ballfield, and the race finish at Central Park.

If you go: The Casino building is gone. Stand at Falmouth Heights Beach and Central Park on Grand Avenue, Downtown side E3, to see where it stood, between the ballfield and the water.

Nearby partner: Holiday Cycles — 465 Grand Avenue, in Falmouth Heights near the beach and the old Casino site.

Learn more: Cape Cod Times, End of an era (Casino demolition, 2003)

St. Anthony’s Church and the strawberry farmers

Portuguese and Cape Verdean farmers in East Falmouth helped fund St. Anthony’s Church, which opened on January 1, 1924, and became known as “the church that strawberries built.”

Off sheet

St. Anthony’s Church

167 East Falmouth Highway, East Falmouth, MA 02536

Off the pull-out map — East Falmouth — On East Falmouth Highway in East Falmouth, east of the illustrated Downtown and Woods Hole sheets.

See this pin on the discovery map

In the late nineteenth and early twentieth centuries, immigrants from the Azores, Portugal, and Cape Verde turned the sandy soil of East Falmouth into a major strawberry-growing district. Farmers along roads such as Davisville shipped berries off Cape, and for a time Falmouth was among the largest strawberry producers in Massachusetts. As early as 1919, strawberry growers began setting aside money from berries picked on a peak Sunday of the harvest — “Strawberry Sunday” — to buy land for a church where Mass could be said in Portuguese. The community appealed to the Diocese of Fall River; in 1921 Rev. Antonio M. Fortuna arrived, and St. Anthony’s Parish was formed. The church at 167 East Falmouth Highway opened on January 1, 1924, and became known as “the church that strawberries built.” In 1977 it ceased to be designated a Portuguese national church and became the parish church for the East Falmouth area, still carrying that farming story in its stained glass and memory.

If you go: St. Anthony’s Church is at 167 East Falmouth Highway, East Falmouth. It is an active parish; check falmouthcatholic.org for Mass times. The church is off the illustrated pull-out map, which covers Downtown–Main Street and Main Street & Woods Hole.

Nearby partner: Route 28 Auto Center — 550 East Falmouth Highway, farther along the same road.

Learn more: St. Anthony’s Church history, Falmouth Catholic Community
